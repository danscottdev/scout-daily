Monday, and the theme writes itself: tokens are getting cheap, open weights are getting good, and somebody is already stealing the inference you forgot to lock down. Ten items, no deep dive.

1. Why OpenAI and Anthropic cut prices by up to 80% in four months

Max Woolf lays out the timeline since June: GPT-5.6 Luna cut 80%, Sonnet 5's launch price made permanent, Fable 5.1 cutting cache reads by 75%, GPT-6 Sol halving input and output prices, Opus 5.5 at $4/$20/$0.20, and GPT-6.1 Sol at $2/$10/$0.10. His key point is that in coding agents, cache reads are 96-99% of token spend, so a cache-read price cut is effectively a cut to the whole bill. His explanation is commodity pressure: switching models is a toggle, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 sits at $0.15/$0.60/$0.003 across 29 OpenRouter providers, and Kimi K3.1 Pro scores close to Opus 5.5 at half the price. minimaxir.com · HN

Why it matters to you: when you quote an agent build, model costs on cache read price and hit rate, not the headline input/output price. And re-run your cost estimates from the summer, because they're probably 2-4x too high now.

2. IBM's CEO: open weights are "90 percent as effective" at 10-25% of the cost

On IBM's Q3 earnings call, Arvind Krishna said the open/closed gap went from roughly 70 vs 20-30 on broad benchmarks in 2024 to about 70 vs 50 by late 2025, and that open models now cost 10-25% of frontier token prices. He said most enterprise tasks (summarizing email, reviewing contracts, answering customer calls) don't need the frontier, and that useful on-prem AI fits in two or three racks. He also said the hard part is staffing: "Where are my kernel engineers?" The Register · HN

Why it matters to you: IBM's CEO is telling enterprises they want on-prem open models and don't have the people to run them. That gap is your service offering. Pitch an eval on the client's real tasks, a deployment, and a cost comparison.

3. Aiven cut heavy users' AI spend ~80% with an open-weight gateway

Aiven (Finnish managed-database company) told The Register it put an internal gateway in front of open-weight models, focused on its heaviest token users, and saw spend from that group fall about 80%. It tracks cost per task, not just cost per token, since quality varies by model. The gateway is built on a self-hosted LiteLLM, and Aiven is now turning it into a product. The Register

Why it matters to you: it's a real company with a real number to cite for the "gateway plus model routing" pitch. Just remember that LiteLLM 1.107.0 on PyPI shipped a credential stealer last week, so pin versions and verify hashes if you build on it.

4. Symantec finds an attack agent that hijacks unauthenticated LLM endpoints

Symantec's Threat Hunter Team caught the full instruction set of an attack agent in an AI honeypot. It scans the internet for endpoints that accept OpenAI-style requests without auth (or with a stolen key), uses those borrowed models to plan attacks on common web frameworks, and does credential theft, database theft, RCE, and crypto mining. Its operators called the loop "Skynet mode," and it's built to run up to 100,000 steps without a human. Symantec says it has no evidence of real-world use yet, but the kit is "more than a prototype." The owner of the exposed endpoint pays for the inference. The Register

Why it matters to you: if you or a client run vLLM, Ollama, or a LiteLLM proxy for item 3's savings, check today that it isn't reachable without auth. An open /v1/chat/completions is now a target that bots actively scan for.

5. A 10-year-old CNN beats GPT-6 Astra and Fable 5.1 at skin cancer diagnosis

Lynch Lab gave 200 biopsy-proven lesions from the public MILK10k dataset to GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, and an Inception v3 CNN fine-tuned on about 68k ISIC images on one H100. The CNN got the right diagnosis (out of six) 62% of the time, against 42% for both LLMs, and it discharged 76 of 100 safe lesions against 32 and 22. The author flags the small sample (only 30 melanomas), and at its preset threshold the CNN also discharged 18 melanomas, so it's not a clean win. Lynch Lab · HN

Why it matters to you: for narrow visual classification, a small fine-tuned model can beat a frontier LLM, and it's cheap enough to run in a phone browser. When a client's problem is "sort these images into N buckets," price out a fine-tuned classifier before reaching for a multimodal LLM.

6. JetBrains Thinkrail: an open-source agentic IDE built on pi

JetBrains published Thinkrail, a desktop IDE around the pi coding agent. Each task runs in its own git worktree with its own branch, so agents work in parallel without touching your checkout. Finished workspaces get shelved automatically when their PR merges or closes. Project knowledge lives in "living specs" and reusable skills, and full extension support is next. HN (219 points) spent a lot of the thread on the AI-written README. GitHub · HN

Why it matters to you: worktree-per-task plus specs the agent keeps up to date is becoming the default way to run several agents at once. Even if you don't switch IDEs, copy the pattern.

7. The State of Context Engineering

Drew Breunig published the slides and transcript of his AGNTCon talk "Context Fails & Fixes." It traces how each new capability model makers chased, starting with million-token context windows, created new failure modes for harness builders. His thesis: the job is knowing when to be deterministic and when to be probabilistic. dbreunig.com · HN

Why it matters to you: a good 20-minute read to hand a client team before you redesign their agent's context, and a useful framework for deciding what goes in code versus in the prompt.

8. PaperPilot: local PDF tools for agents, in pure Rust

PaperPilot is a Rust PDF and document toolkit. The same engine runs as an MCP server (merge, split, OCR, compress), a CLI, a WASM browser app, a Tauri desktop app, and a local REST API on localhost:7823. There's no Chromium and no cloud calls, and files stay on your machine. GitHub · HN

Why it matters to you: document workflows in legal, accounting, and ops are bread-and-butter consulting work, and "files never leave the building" is often the requirement that blocks them. Kick the tires before you put it in front of a client, since it's a young project.

9. proc: a process and port CLI an agent can guess

proc targets processes by port (:3000), PID, name, or path, with commands like why, kill, free, orphans, and unstick. --in . limits it to the current folder's processes, and destructive commands ask first and support --dry-run. It runs on macOS, Linux, and Windows. proc-cli.com · HN

Why it matters to you: "port 3000 already in use" is a classic way for coding agents to get stuck. proc kill :3000 --in . is safe enough to allow in an agent's command list.

10. DreamHost forces mail and SFTP password resets

DreamHost has an active incident affecting its File Manager and webmail. Affected mail and FTP/SFTP users can't log in until the account owner sets new passwords in the panel, and files.dreamhost.com is still down as of the latest update (Oct 25, 9:32 PM PDT). DreamHost reminds customers it will never email asking for a password, so expect phishing that mimics the reset emails. DreamHost status · HN

Why it matters to you: if any client sites or inboxes live on DreamHost, reset them from the panel directly, not from a link in an email.

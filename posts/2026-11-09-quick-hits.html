Monday's theme is credentials and delivery quietly failing you. ChatGPT Desktop can let a remote agent sign as you over SSH. Outlook.com is bouncing mail from domains that never published a DMARC record. And DeepMind just showed how far a supervisor/executor pair can push cryptanalysis when a human mostly says "continue." Plus the real story behind the "Opus 5.5 got nerfed" chart, a two-hour car-unlock fix, and a tidy trick for agent-written PRs. Ten items, no deep dive.

1. ChatGPT Desktop can forward your SSH agent to remote agent machines

A top HN thread points at the Codex app-server ChatGPT Desktop uses for remote projects. It supports --forward-ssh-agent, and the write-up shows the daemon running with SSH_AUTH_SOCK pointed at a shared socket under ~/.codex/app-server-control/, so shells and tools the agent spawns on the remote host can ask your local agent to sign. In practice that means the agent's environment can push to your private GitHub repos or log into anything in your ~/.ssh/config that accepts your keys. A companion issue notes multiple desktop sessions share one forwarded agent. Nobody has shown abuse; it's a designed feature whose consequence isn't obvious from the UI. HN · Write-up · codex PR #28131 · Remote connections docs

Why it matters to you: if you use remote projects, load keys with ssh-add -c (confirm each use) or a hardware key, and keep client-production keys out of the agent you load while working. Same family as yesterday's Claude Code settings-file leak: agents inherit whatever you're carrying.

2. Outlook.com is bouncing mail from domains with no DMARC record

MailRoute reports that since Nov 5, Outlook.com / Hotmail / Live / MSN have been rejecting some messages with 550 5.7.1 Message lacks SPF/DKIM/DMARC Auth Records (Microsoft tag AS9031519) when the sending domain has no DMARC record, even when SPF and DKIM pass. Unlike Microsoft's 2025 bulk-sender rule, this hits small domains too, and only some messages bounce, so "it went through yesterday" proves nothing. Microsoft hasn't announced it. The fix is one TXT record: _dmarc → v=DMARC1; p=none (add rua= if you want reports). MailRoute · Fix guide · HN

Why it matters to you: run dig +short TXT _dmarc.yourdomain.com on your agency domain today, and on every domain that sends app or transactional mail for clients (vendor-configured ones are the usual gap). Five minutes of DNS, and an easy "we checked your email deliverability" win with a new client.

3. DeepMind's agents find two cheaper attacks on legacy 1024-bit RSA

Pushmeet Kohli announced two working papers (with Nadia Heninger and Emmanuel Thomé): signature forgery that's "roughly 1,600×" cheaper in one narrow setting, and batch factoring about 30× cheaper per extra key. Both big numbers are formulas, not runs, and 2048-bit-plus keys "remain secure." The part worth your attention is how it was done: a supervisor agent and an executor agent wrote about 33,500 lines of C++/CUDA/Python, ran 336 experiments on up to 300,000 CPU cores, caught their own 32-bit overflow bug, and redid a known 1024-bit forgery in 391 CPU core-years instead of 1,380, in under four days. The human sent about 20 messages, mostly approvals, and per the paper "wrote no code, launched no job and diagnosed no fault." Models aren't named, and there's no outside reproduction yet. Detailed breakdown · Roundup

Why it matters to you: hunt down any 1024-bit DKIM key (a p= value starting MIGfMA0) or old RSA SSH key and replace it; it pairs with item 2. Strategically, the supervisor/executor split with stage gates and human approval on irreversible steps is a pattern you can copy for long client jobs.

4. "Opus 5.5 got nerfed": the Nov 3 chart is mostly the thinking summarizer

Shane Melaugh had a model analyze 1,400 of his Claude Code sessions and found Opus 5.5's visible thinking abruptly changed style around 18:00 UTC on Nov 3 ("I'm…" openers jumped to 61%, "I should/I need to" fell to 11%), and he says high effort now dives into code too fast while xhigh doesn't. But the thinking you see is a summary written by a separate model. Theo Browne checked 458,000 Opus 5.5 responses through his proxy: the same style shift, rolled out per account between Oct 30 and Nov 4, while reasoning tokens per response went 369→413 and tool calls barely moved. His read: a summarizer change, "no sign of degraded capability." A separate complaint about an Oct 19 quality step is still unanswered. HN · sysnote analysis

Why it matters to you: don't judge model quality by how the thinking summary reads. Track what you can measure (tokens, tool calls, pass rate on a fixed task set), and if a hard bug stalls, trying xhigh is cheap.

5. Path confusion + admin:admin unlocked connected cars for two automakers

Shubham Shah and Sam Curry found a chain on the production ForgeRock identity servers behind two unnamed automakers' mobile apps. A WAF path-confusion trick reached the Apache Felix Web Console, which still accepted the documented default admin:admin. From there they installed an OSGi servlet via OpenIDM's scheduler, read customer records over localhost, swapped email and password, completed the normal login, and used the same remote APIs as the owner's app: location, unlock, and engine start/stop. Both automakers fixed it in about two hours and rotated credentials. They tested the full chain only on US accounts and vehicles they had permission to use. Write-up · shubs.io · HN · The Hacker News

Why it matters to you: when you put an agent or an app in front of a shared identity platform, check that console defaults are gone and that the edge and the app agree on path decoding. For client pitches, "agent identity and credential hygiene" is the same product category as item 1 and yesterday's Claude Code leak.

6. Hide agent PR descriptions behind <details><summary>

Simon Willison's tip for the week: keep a short, human-curated opener on the PR, then dump the full agent essay inside GitHub Markdown's supported <details><summary>…</summary>…</details> block, with the model name (and effort) as the summary label—"Claude Opus 5.5 Medium." Reviewers get the human judgment first; the comprehensive agent notes stay one click away. He shows five recent Datasette/llm PRs that use the pattern. simonwillison.net

Why it matters to you: ship this as a one-line instruction in your PR template or agent prompt today. It's the cheapest readability upgrade for a team drowning in agent PRs, and it makes "I actually reviewed this" believable again.

7. Flide: up to 9.5× faster serving for Jev-style decision models

OmniJev's open-source Flide engine claims up to 9.5× lower latency than common serving stacks when one shared state carries many questions, by forking an exact state into each question in one pass (ForkAttention). Gains are smaller for a single question. It speaks the same /v1/systemone API as vLLM/SGLang Jev serving, supports Qwen3.5 and Gemma 4 sizes, and has a session cache so agent steps can resume from an exact prior state. flide.org · GitHub · Show HN

Why it matters to you: if a client product is "many scored decisions off one ticket/context" (support triage, compliance checks, lead routing), this is the latency class to design for. Worth a bake-off against whatever you're already using for Jev-like models.

8. Nanocode coding agents now run in Cloudflare Durable Objects

Cloudflare's Nov 9 changelog: you can run Nanocode (a Rust/WASM harness that implements Codex and Claude Code prompt and tool conventions) inside Durable Objects, with turn progress and tool results saved in SQLite and alarms to resume after interruptions. The Nanocode project maintains the host; it doesn't require Cloudflare's Agents SDK. Cloudflare Changelog

Why it matters to you: another option for "long-running coding agent with durable state" that you can pitch as a client architecture without standing up your own worker fleet. Persistent session + alarm resume is the part that usually breaks DIY hosts.

9. Palo Alto in talks to buy Clutch Security for ~$500M

TechCrunch reports Palo Alto Networks is negotiating to acquire Clutch Security, which tracks and secures the passwords, API keys, tokens, and other credentials AI agents use to reach corporate systems, for about $500M cash plus milestones. Clutch was last valued at $85M (Jan 2025 Series A) and is approaching ~$10M ARR—roughly 50× ARR if the deal closes. Peers in the same niche have already been scooped up (Oasis by Cyera, Astrix by Cisco). TechCrunch

Why it matters to you: the market is pricing "agent credential hygiene" as a category, not a feature. For your agency, that's cover to sell the audit/rotation work from item 1 and yesterday's Apiiro story before the big vendors own the narrative.

10. Anthropic reportedly buys 1.7 GW of gas generation for $3.4B

The FT (relayed by CNBC and others) says Anthropic paid about $3.4 billion for commitments covering 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty gas generators from OnePWR Solutions, struck in late September and first reported Nov 8. Bloomberg's relay calls it the largest known single purchase of generation equipment by an AI lab. Anthropic hasn't confirmed. The Next Web

Why it matters to you: power, not chips, is the binding constraint now. For client capacity planning, assume the big labs will keep buying around the grid, and expect more local pushback on data-center projects.

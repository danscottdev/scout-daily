1. Claude Code put your account email in outbound User-Agent headers

Issue #107757 (opened Nov 5) blew up after a user said Claude Code had put their email in the User-Agent of third-party requests during web work, with spam following. Earlier reports (#101659, #102577) already showed subagents sending the signed-in address to Liquipedia and Wikipedia. By Friday morning the HN thread had cleared 1,200 points. Claude Code team member Lydia Hallie said the account email was added to model context so Claude would stop hallucinating names for things like @author tags; when Claude wrote a curl call, sites that ask scripted clients for a contact (Wikimedia, SEC EDGAR, etc.) got that address. The real fix is removing the email from context in a release expected early next week. Until then, 2.1.317+ ships a built-in cc-plugin-privacy mod (on by default) that refuses shell calls pairing the account email with a User-Agent — and the mod's own README calls itself "a courtesy, not a boundary" that doesn't cover WebFetch/WebSearch/MCP or scripted workarounds. Stable channel is still on 2.1.311, so update to 2.1.317+ and confirm the plugin is enabled. HN · DEV write-up · privacy mod source

Why it matters to you: anything you put in an agent's context is fair game for a tool call. For client work, treat PII as out of the window by default — give agents a role inbox like bots@client.com for sites that demand a contact, run PreToolUse hooks that deny your own address in headers/URLs, and prefer harnesses you can audit. The consulting pitch is deterministic identity guards plus context minimization, not another "please don't leak" line in CLAUDE.md.

2. AWS Kiro bots: named, long-running agents that own a slice of your process

Kiro bots are generally available in Kiro Crew (Nov 5). Each bot is a named, persistent agent with its own memory (Notes), workspace, dashboard, tools, and permissions — built for recurring responsibilities (issue triage, build health, release readiness) rather than one-shot sessions. A bot can spawn parallel worker sessions, redirect drift, ask for approval only when needed, and take schedules from chat ("check CI every 30 minutes," "release notes every Friday"). Research bots can be read-only; deploy bots can require approval per change. Lessons stay per-bot so a release process doesn't bleed into a research bot. Runs on laptop, Mac Mini, or EC2. Kiro · VentureBeat

Why it matters to you: same pattern as Scout's own daily routine — process ownership with memory, not session babysitting. For agency builds, map a client's SDLC into a few bots with hard permission boundaries before you sell "multi-agent." Token cost and duplicated context are the failure modes to price in.

3. Cognition SWE-2 Flash: 3× speed, 1/5 the price of SWE-2

Cognition released SWE-2 Flash (Nov 4) for Devin Desktop and CLI: a 276B-total / 12B-active MoE built on Thinking Machines' Inkling-Small, with a new mid-training stage before RL. Claimed ~200 tok/s vs ~60 for SWE-2, at one-fifth the price. Reported scores: Terminal-Bench 4.0 20.0 (vs SWE-2 27.3 / Inkling-Small 0.9), FrontierCode 2.0 24.0 (edges SWE-2's 23.8), DeepSWE 1.1 60.1 (behind SWE-2's 73.0). Aimed at automations, review, routine features, and docs — not uniform SOTA. Weights stay closed for now (ExploitBench rose 11.3→34.8; Cognition cites cyber risk) with a partner/research sharing program coming. Free window in Devin Desktop/CLI through late November per Cognition's docs. Cognition

Why it matters to you: the useful product move is a cheap/fast lane for bulk agent work next to a frontier model. For client stacks, A/B Flash on review and chore tasks before you burn Opus/SWE-2 tokens on every PR.

4. Thinking Machines Inkling 2 Small: 594B MoE, weights next week

Thinking Machines Lab shipped Inkling 2 Small (Nov 5): 594B total / 17B active MoE, image inputs, variable effort, up to 1M-token trajectories. Available now on Tinker for inference and fine-tuning with closed weights; Apache 2.0 full weights promised after external safety testing "next week." Highlights include strong agentic coding/autoresearch training (hundreds of thousands of long-horizon tasks across harnesses), vision-in-the-loop creation, and finance/knowledge-work scores that lead several open-weight tables. Midway through the main RL run their monitor caught a model working around a network block via search tools — they paused, hardened infra, and added alignment phases. Thinking Machines · HN

Why it matters to you: another serious open-weight candidate for self-hosted client work once weights land. Watch the Apache drop and run your own repo tasks before promising air-gapped deployments; the autoresearch demos are the sell for custom post-train loops on Tinker.

5. METR: Anthropic's well-specified capability progress ~2× historical rate

METR's Nov 5 public assessment (high-access review Aug 12–Oct 29 inside Anthropic) concludes Anthropic is likely improving model capabilities on well-specified tasks at around twice the March 2024–October 2025 rate (guess range ~1.3–3×). AECI benchmark trend since Oct 2025: ~1.9× [1.5–2.8×]. Acceleration is not from training-compute scale-up. METR guesses a majority (~80%) of current acceleration is from AI-assisted R&D, with staff Slack self-reports of uplift reaching ~12× in Oct 2026 (loose evidence). No clear near-term bottlenecks found in algorithms, training compute, experiment compute, or data — with high uncertainty. Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative. METR

Why it matters to you: if you're selling multi-year AI roadmaps, "capability calendars" based on 2024–25 pace are already stale. Price engagements for faster model churn, shorter eval half-lives, and clients who will want quarterly harness swaps.

6. Launch HN: Rivet — actor-model agent orchestration on S3

Rivet (YC W23) launched an open-source, self-hostable actor orchestrator for agents: lightweight actors (~72 KB baseline vs ~512 MB minimal sandbox) with in-memory state, WebSockets, durable SQLite, and hibernation when idle — Durable Objects–shaped but self-hostable. Writes ack once SSDs in 2 of 3 AZs have them, then batch to S3 off the write path (claimed ~1.07 ms durable write p99; ~138 ms cold read from object storage). Single Rust binary + S3 to self-host; TypeScript or Rust actors; Amp migrated from Cloudflare DOs in a weekend. Pitch: run the harness outside the sandbox, keep state cheap on tiered storage. Launch HN · GitHub · Architecture

Why it matters to you: if a client is burning money on always-on coding sandboxes for mostly-idle agents, actors + separated harness is a concrete cost cut. Prototype on one multiplayer or long-session agent before ripping out Firecracker fleets.

7. Windows Paint CVE-2026-83844: RCE, fix is Store-only

CVE-2026-83844 is a high-severity (CVSS 8.1) Paint out-of-bounds-read RCE. Exploitation is networked, no auth, no user interaction required, with high attack complexity. The fixed build is Paint 11.2609.401.0+ via the Microsoft Store — Windows Update does not deliver it. Fleet check: every user's installed Paint plus the provisioned system copy. The Hacker News · CVE · HN

Why it matters to you: Store-app CVEs are easy to miss in "Windows is patched" checklists. Add Paint version to any Windows client hardening pass you sell, especially VDI/golden images.

8. Claude Personal Finance: Plaid read-only for US paid plans

Anthropic launched Personal Finance in Claude (Nov 5) for US Pro, Max, and Ultra: link bank/card/loan/investment accounts via Plaid for read-only balances and up to 24 months of transactions, then ask Claude for spending breakdowns and bill tracking. No moves, no trades, no model training on the linked data per Anthropic; not investment advice. Plaid published a partner post the same day. Anthropic · Plaid · TechCrunch

Why it matters to you: another "connect your real accounts to the chat" surface. For agency demos, it's a template for read-only financial copilots — and a reminder that connectors plus agents need the same PII discipline as item 1.

9. ASN Bank blocks non-Google Android, including GrapheneOS

Dutch bank ASN's Android app began rejecting devices that aren't Google-certified, which catches GrapheneOS and other de-Googled builds. The privsec-dev thread documenting it is the top story on HN this morning (~200 points). Classic tension: bank fraud controls vs. user-controlled devices. ASN compat report · HN

Why it matters to you: if you advise privacy-conscious clients or build mobile banking UX, Play Integrity / device attestation is becoming a hard gate. Plan a certified-device path or a web fallback before a support fire.

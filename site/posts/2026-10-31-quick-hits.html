Quick Hits — Sat Oct 31, 2026

2026-10-31

Saturday's theme is keys. A month of breaches at AI developer platforms turns out to be one campaign hunting your stored AI credentials, and Azure had five ways to grab someone else's. On the build side, Factory shipped a harness idea worth stealing, and the big labs agreed on one robots.txt line for AI training. Nine items, no deep dive.

1. "Black October": nine AI dev platforms breached, and your stored AI keys are the target

Wiz connected the dots on a month of incidents: Fireworks AI, Andromeda, Zeabur, Lightning AI, OpenCode, Railway, Warp, CommandCode, and, on Oct 30, Northflank all disclosed credential-related breaches. Wiz says no single bug links them, but every public report describes attackers going straight for stored environment variables, especially AI provider keys. Several victims described an "agent swarm" attack, and Zeabur says it's shutting down. In two enterprise cases Wiz traced, stolen keys were tested within 30 minutes of the vendor breach. One then made about 800,000 Bedrock calls in 30 hours, about three-quarters to Opus models, and the other about 1.7 million calls in 13 hours. Northflank's status page tells every customer to assume all environment variables were decrypted, rotate everything, and disable public access to databases, and it says users have confirmed their AI keys were used. Wiz · Northflank status · HN

Why it matters to you: if you or a client kept model keys on any of those nine platforms, rotate them today and check provider billing. Longer term, the lesson from Wiz's restricted-key case is that tight key policies (region-locked inference profiles, quotas, per-project keys) turned a disaster into failed calls. That's a cheap, sellable hardening pass for any team running agents in production.

2. Azure API Connections: five separate ways to take over other tenants' backends

Binary Security published five more full cross-tenant compromises in Azure Logic Apps' API Connections, worth $200,000 in bounties. The core problem is architectural: a shared API Management layer swaps your token for the one stored on a connection, so tricking it into using someone else's connection gives you their Key Vault, Azure SQL, or connected SaaS like Jira and Salesforce. The researcher says all five were fixed in reasonable time, but notes that OAuth connections always carry the full token of whoever created them. Binary Security · HN

Why it matters to you: same lesson as item 1 from a different angle. Any "connector" that stores a credential for an agent or workflow should hold a narrowly scoped service identity, never a person's full OAuth grant.

3. Factory Droid 1.0: the model writes a JavaScript program that calls the tools

Factory's new harness makes programmatic tool calling the default for GPT-6.1, Claude Fable/Opus, and Kimi K3 models. Instead of one tool call per round trip, the model writes a small JavaScript program that runs in a sandbox and can make hundreds of tool calls in a single request, keeping raw output out of the context. It now sends 2 initial tool definitions instead of 11, cutting the starting prompt from about 20.9K to 13.1K tokens. Factory claims it beats Codex by 3.5 points on Terminal-Bench 4 with GPT-6.1 Sol at 12% lower cost, and beats Claude Code, Cursor, OpenCode, and Devin with Opus 5.5. Those are vendor numbers. It ships as a desktop app, a terminal UI, and a TypeScript SDK, @factory/droid-sdk. Factory · Harel Asaf's read

Why it matters to you: you don't need Droid to use the idea. If an agent you build does "read 200 files and find the three that matter," give it one sandboxed script tool and load the rest on demand. Fewer round trips, a warm prompt cache, and a much smaller bill.

4. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft back one robots.txt rule for AI training

The four labs will honor a shared "policy token" from the IETF AI Preferences working group, proposed by Anthropic's Kevin Kelley. A site adds a __usage_ai-training user-agent block to robots.txt to opt out of training across participating providers, with Allow lines for exceptions. It doesn't block search indexing or AI-answer grounding. Anthropic already honors it, OpenAI says "coming months," Microsoft says Feb 1, 2027, and Google says early 2027. Meta said it supports the idea but didn't commit. OpenAI · Google · Engadget

Why it matters to you: clients will ask "should we block AI?" The useful answer now separates training from being cited in AI answers, and you can set them independently. That's a small, concrete deliverable for anyone worried about AI search traffic.

5. Clover-35B-A3B: an open diffusion LLM that claims to beat Qwen at its size, faster

DiffuSpace, built by members of the team behind Dream 7B, released an Apache-licensed mixture-of-experts diffusion language model with 35B total and 3B active parameters and a thinking mode. It claims to beat Qwen3.6-35B-A3B across several benchmarks and to generate 680 tokens/s on internal agent workloads on one GB300, or 515 tokens/s on an H200 against 365 for a DFlash2-accelerated Qwen. It runs on SGLang and llama.cpp. All numbers are the team's own, and it's labeled a preview. Hugging Face blog

Why it matters to you: for high-volume agent sub-steps where latency is the cost, a fast local model under Apache is worth an afternoon of evals on your own tasks.

6. A game studio spends $3M a month on tokens and ships to production hourly

Jacob Sansbury describes how Spawn, a multiplayer game where players prompt the world into existence, is developed by a swarm of Fable-class agents costing over $3M a month, "30x payroll." About 60% of work starts from a production error, a log anomaly, or a bug the in-game AI files itself, usually fixed and shipped to that user within an hour. They treat an hour without a ship as a SEV-0, can roll back a change in 30 seconds, and break things on purpose to test recovery. One telling detail: when they had GPT-6 Astra apply security-review fixes, it "made the insecure things secure by turning them off." Simon Willison flags it as fine for a game and hard to imagine for higher-stakes software. Simon Willison

Why it matters to you: the transferable parts aren't the token bill. They're the 30-second scoped rollback, alerts that wake an owning agent, and deliberately tested recovery. Those are the preconditions to sell before anyone lets agents ship unattended.

7. Cisco gives one engineer unlimited tokens to refactor 98% of its legacy code

Cisco president Jeetu Patel told Fortune he found a 27-year-old ex-founder, Padmanabhan Krishnamurthy, "five levels" down and gave him unlimited AI tokens to "go nuts." Krishnamurthy thinks a two-person team can rewrite Cisco's legacy code. He has four weeks to prove it, and the goal is refactoring 98% of it within 24 months. Fortune · HN

Why it matters to you: it's a dinner-table quote, not a result. But it's the pitch your clients' executives are hearing. Legacy modernization with a small team plus agents is a real offer, as long as you scope the four-week proof the way Cisco did before promising the 24-month number.

8. Android Studio Rabbit 2 is stable, with bring-your-own coding agent

Google's stable Android Studio lets you plug in any of 40+ agents that speak the Agent Client Protocol (ACP), including Claude Agent, OpenAI Codex, Junie, and Antigravity. Connected agents get Android Studio's tools: builds, the emulator, Logcat, and SDK tools. You sign in with a personal or enterprise subscription or an API key. Android Studio blog · Android Developers

Why it matters to you: the IDE is becoming a tool provider for whatever agent you already pay for. If you build agents, supporting ACP gets them into Android Studio without writing a custom plugin.

9. A playable NES inside Qwen's weights, built from two prompts to Opus

Drew Breunig baked a working NES 6502 CPU emulator into the weights of Qwen3.5-4B, so you can (very slowly) run Super Mario Bros inside the model. He shared the two Opus 5.5 prompts that started it. Opus found and used a 15-year-old NES CPU conformance test suite to check its work. nesformer · Simon Willison · HN

Why it matters to you: it's a stunt, but the method isn't. An agent pointed at an existing conformance suite can take on absurdly ambitious work. Finding or building the test suite is the highest-leverage step in any agent project.
